===================================================
SOFTWARE QUALITY ASSURANCE (SQA) — SUMMARY NOTES
===================================================

WHAT IS SOFTWARE QUALITY ASSURANCE (SQA)?
- SQA is a way to assure quality in software.
- It's a set of activities that ensure processes, procedures,
  and standards are suitable for the project and correctly
  implemented.
- Works IN PARALLEL with software development (not after it).
- Focuses on improving the development PROCESS itself, so
  problems are prevented before becoming major issues.
- Described as an "umbrella activity" — applied throughout the
  entire software process, not just at one stage.

QUALITY (GENERAL CONCEPT)
- Quality in a product/service is defined by several measurable
  characteristics, each playing a role in overall quality.
- Software quality is often verified by third-party
  organizations, such as international standards organizations
  (e.g., ISO).


===================================================
1. ELEMENTS OF SOFTWARE QUALITY ASSURANCE
===================================================

- STANDARDS
  - Organizations like IEEE and ISO provide software
    engineering standards and related documents.
  - SQA's job: ensure adopted standards are followed and all
    work products conform to them.

- REVIEWS AND AUDITS
  - Technical reviews: a quality control activity done BY
    software engineers FOR software engineers, aimed at
    uncovering errors.
  - Audits: reviews performed by SQA personnel to ensure
    quality guidelines are being followed in engineering work.

- TESTING
  - A quality control function whose main goal is finding
    errors.
  - SQA ensures testing is properly planned and efficiently
    conducted.

- ERROR/DEFECT COLLECTION AND ANALYSIS
  - SQA collects and analyzes error/defect data to understand
    how errors are introduced, and which practices best
    eliminate them.

- CHANGE MANAGEMENT
  - SQA ensures adequate change management practices are in
    place.

- EDUCATION
  - Improving software engineering practices relies heavily on
    educating engineers, managers, and stakeholders.
  - SQA leads software process improvement and sponsors
    educational programs.

- SECURITY MANAGEMENT
  - SQA ensures the right processes and technologies are used
    to achieve software security.

- SAFETY
  - SQA may assess the impact of software failure and initiate
    steps to reduce associated risk.

- RISK MANAGEMENT
  - SQA ensures risk management activities are properly
    conducted and that risk-related contingency plans exist.


===================================================
2. FOCUS AREAS OF SOFTWARE QUALITY ASSURANCE
===================================================

- PORTABILITY
  - The software's ability to be easily transferred/adapted to
    different environments or platforms without major changes.
  - Improves accessibility and flexibility across systems.

- USABILITY
  - How easy and intuitive the software is to use and navigate.
  - High usability = users accomplish tasks with minimal
    confusion/frustration → positive user experience.

- REUSABILITY
  - Designing components/modules that can be reused across
    different parts of the software or in other projects.
  - Reduces development time and boosts productivity and
    maintainability.

- CORRECTNESS
  - The software's ability to produce the desired results under
    specific conditions/inputs.
  - Correct software behaves as expected, meeting its defined
    requirements and specifications.

- MAINTAINABILITY
  - How easily software can be modified, updated, or extended
    over time.
  - Well-maintained software is structured/documented so
    developers can make changes efficiently without introducing
    errors or destabilizing the system.

- ERROR CONTROL
  - Mechanisms to detect, handle, and gracefully recover from
    errors or unexpected situations.
  - Keeps the software robust and reliable, minimizing
    disruption to users.


===================================================
3. MAJOR ACTIVITIES IN SOFTWARE QUALITY ASSURANCE
===================================================

1. SQA MANAGEMENT PLAN
   - Plan how SQA will be carried out throughout the project.
   - Decide which software engineering activities best suit the
     project; check the SQA team's skill level.

2. SET THE CHECKPOINTS
   - Establish checkpoints to evaluate project performance based
     on data collected at each stage.

3. MEASURE CHANGE IMPACT
   - Fixing an error can sometimes introduce new ones — track
     the impact of each change on the project.
   - Re-test new changes to confirm compatibility with the whole
     system.

4. MULTI-TESTING STRATEGY
   - Don't rely on a single testing approach — use multiple
     testing methods when available.

5. MANAGE GOOD RELATIONS
   - Maintaining good relationships between the SQA team and
     other teams (e.g., programmers) is essential.
   - Poor relations directly and negatively affect the project.

6. MAINTAINING RECORDS AND REPORTS
   - Thoroughly document and share QA records — test cases,
     defects, changes, and cycles — for stakeholder awareness
     and future reference.

7. REVIEW SOFTWARE ENGINEERING ACTIVITIES
   - The SQA group identifies and documents processes, and
     verifies the correctness of the software product.

8. FORMALIZE DEVIATION HANDLING
   - Carefully track and document software deviations.
   - Follow established procedures for handling variances.


===================================================
4. BENEFITS OF SOFTWARE QUALITY ASSURANCE
===================================================

- Produces high-quality software.
- High-quality applications save time and cost.
- Leads to better reliability.
- Reduces the need for long-term maintenance.
- High-quality commercial software increases a company's market
  share.
- Improves the overall process of creating software.
- Improves the quality of the software itself.
- Cuts maintenance costs — getting the release right the first
  time lets a company move on to the next project, while chronic
  issues trap a business in a costly, endless repair cycle.


===================================================
5. DISADVANTAGES OF SOFTWARE QUALITY ASSURANCE
===================================================

- COST
  - Requires more resources, increasing the project budget.

- TIME-CONSUMING
  - Testing and deployment take more time, which can delay the
    project.

- OVERHEAD
  - Introduces administrative overhead — documentation,
    reporting, and tracking quality metrics.
  - This burden can sometimes outweigh the benefits, especially
    for smaller projects.

- RESOURCE INTENSIVE
  - Requires skilled personnel experienced in testing
    methodologies, tools, and QA practices.
  - Finding and retaining such talent can be difficult and
    costly.

- RESISTANCE TO CHANGE
  - Some team members may see SQA processes as bureaucratic or
    unnecessary, resisting their adoption.
  - This can hinder how effectively QA practices take hold in an
    organization.

- NOT FOOLPROOF
  - Even with thorough testing and QA, software can still
    contain defects or vulnerabilities.
  - SQA cannot guarantee the total elimination of bugs/issues.

- COMPLEXITY
  - SQA processes can become complex in large-scale projects
    with many stakeholders, dependencies, and integration points.
  - Managing this complexity requires careful planning and
    coordination.


===================================================
CONCLUSION
===================================================
Software Quality Assurance is a continuous, parallel process
throughout software development — not a single final check.
It combines standards, reviews, testing, risk management, and
education to prevent problems early, and while it comes with
real costs (time, resources, overhead), it plays a critical
role in the overall success of software projects.
